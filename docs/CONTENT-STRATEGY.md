# Edu Hub Content Strategy

## Purpose

Edu Hub content exists to help parents and students make better education decisions while strengthening the authority and discoverability of the structured directory.

The editorial system is not an article-volume engine.

## Audience priority

1. Parents
2. Students

Secondary audiences include institutions, educators, consultants and advertisers, but Phase 1 content should primarily solve parent/student questions.

## Content pillars

Initial pillars:

- Choosing education
- Curricula and education systems
- Admissions
- Fees and costs
- Parent guides
- Student guides
- Universities and higher education
- Programs, majors and careers
- Exams and academic pathways
- Education policy/developments
- Scholarships
- Special education

These are strategic clusters, not final keyword categories.

## Content types

- guide
- explainer
- comparison
- article
- report
- relevant education news/update

## Directory + editorial relationship

Editorial content should connect to structured entities whenever useful.

Examples:

```text
British curriculum guide
→ British curriculum hub
→ relevant school profiles

University admissions guide
→ universities
→ programs
→ admission cycles

Fees guide
→ fee comparison/structured fee data
→ relevant institutions
```

Do not create artificial links purely for keyword repetition.

## Research hierarchy

Prioritize sources in this order:

1. Government/regulator
2. Official institution website/document
3. Official accreditation/curriculum authority
4. Official institution social channel when it is the primary publication channel
5. Other authoritative primary/official source
6. Trusted secondary sources when necessary

Important factual claims should preserve source references where practical.

## AI editorial workflow

Target workflow:

```text
Opportunity
→ intent/keyword research
→ existing-content check
→ source research
→ evidence extraction
→ brief
→ AI-assisted draft
→ claim/fact check
→ original-value review
→ internal-link plan
→ Arabic/English localization
→ SEO review
→ editorial approval
→ publish
→ monitor
→ refresh
```

AI must not autonomously publish unsupported factual content during the initial release.

## Editorial states

Recommended lifecycle:

- idea
- researching
- brief_ready
- draft
- fact_check
- editorial_review
- translation/localization
- seo_review
- scheduled
- published
- refresh_due
- archived

## Bilingual strategy

Arabic and English are first-class experiences tied to the same underlying entities but not forced into literal translations.

Rules:

- preserve factual equivalence
- localize phrasing/search intent where justified
- review Arabic for natural editorial quality
- support RTL intentionally
- allow one locale to remain draft if its quality is not ready rather than publishing poor translation

## Content quality test

Before publishing, ask:

- Does this answer a real parent/student question?
- Does it contain useful information beyond generic summaries?
- Are material factual claims supported?
- Does it connect naturally to relevant directory data?
- Is it meaningfully distinct from existing Edu Hub content?
- Would it still be worth publishing if search traffic were lower than expected?

If not, do not publish solely to create another URL.

## Freshness

High-volatility editorial topics such as admissions deadlines, fees, exams and policy changes need explicit review/refresh logic.

Evergreen content should still be reviewed when underlying regulations, curricula, institutions or cited facts change.

## Initial editorial research backlog

Before large-scale publishing, conduct search-demand research around:

- major school locations in Cairo/Giza/Alexandria
- British/American/IB/national education systems
- school fees and admissions
- university categories and admissions
- programs/majors with meaningful demand
- parent decision questions
- Arabic vs English query differences

The output should inform the first indexable location/curriculum landing pages and editorial calendar.
